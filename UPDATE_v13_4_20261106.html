New publications since the previous source review

  • P125 · What’s the ROI of software engineers’ spending on AI?
    Gergely Orosz · 2026-10-20

    Reporting from 27 workplaces, combined with CodeRabbit and Gitar aggregates, describes wide variation in AI spending and changes in pull-request throughput. Public examples include higher spending with little throughput change as well as large output increases. The piece supplies practitioner context for spending limits and for measuring benefits beyond PR counts; it does not establish a causal return on AI spending.

  • P126 · AI SRE: what is it, how could it change on-call – and does it work?
    Gergely Orosz; Jessica Salmon · 2026-10-27

    Interviews with Rootly, incident.io and ResolveAI describe agents that gather incident context, investigate hypotheses and support engineers in collaboration tools. Public sections distinguish investigation from autonomous remediation, explain service graphs and memory, and discuss data-access and trust constraints. The piece illustrates operational agent design rather than independently benchmarking these tools.

  • P127 · How software engineering is changing: a tale of two eras
    Neil Kakkar (guest essay); Gergely Orosz, Jessica Salmon and Ivan Klaric (editorial byline) · 2026-11-03

    Neil Kakkar compares his own work at an AI-native startup with earlier employers and reports roughly eleven times as many merged PRs as at his previous startup. Alongside higher output, his account describes production errors, context-switching fatigue and loss of shared knowledge as review practices and ownership change. Editorial commentary distinguishes iteration speed from business results.

  • F040 · Why are good developers so wary of agentic development?
    Rachel Laycock · 2026-10-29

    Laycock interprets experienced developers’ caution as concern for production behavior and long-term changeability. An exploratory agent-built airline application shifted human work toward domain design and harnesses, while persistent build failures exposed unresolved coordination and verification problems. She calls for experienced engineers to help test emerging practices, and for leaders to evaluate trustworthy delivery rather than generated code volume.

  • F041 · LLM Voice
    Martin Fowler · 2026-10-29

    Fowler argues that AI-generated prose can weaken readers’ willingness to engage and can displace the thinking that happens during writing. He recommends retaining an author’s own voice and disclosing AI assistance, while acknowledging uncertainty in recognizing generated text and exceptions for accessibility, translation and routine process communications.

Older articles newly catalogued

  • O052 · Your parallel Agent limit
    Addy Osmani · 2026-04-07

    Osmani argues that parallel agent work is constrained by a person’s capacity to understand, evaluate and integrate separate tasks. He recommends adjusting concurrency to task complexity and review quality, defining time limits and acceptance criteria, and reducing per-task scope when supervision becomes shallow.

  • O053 · Agentic Engine Optimization (AEO)
    Addy Osmani · 2026-04-11

    Osmani proposes adapting developer documentation and interfaces to agents that fetch and consume content directly. His recommendations emphasize discoverable, concise, machine-readable documentation, clear capability descriptions and server-side observation of usage. He presents documentation indexes, skills and tool interfaces as possible components rather than a single solution.

  • O054 · Agent Harness Engineering
    Addy Osmani · 2026-04-19

    The article treats an agent’s tools, execution environment, durable state, context policies, checks and recovery paths as engineering artifacts surrounding the model. Osmani recommends deriving these controls from desired behavior and observed failures, and using deterministic enforcement and structured handoffs where prose instructions are insufficient.

  • O055 · Agent Skills
    Addy Osmani · 2026-05-03

    Osmani explains the design of his Agent Skills project as reusable engineering workflows with checkpoints and explicit completion evidence. The article connects specifications, scoped implementation, testing and review with selective loading of instructions and written responses to common reasons for skipping steps. It describes a design approach that teams can adapt.

  • O056 · Cognitive Surrender
    Addy Osmani · 2026-05-05

    Osmani distinguishes delegating execution while retaining judgment from adopting an AI answer without forming an independent view. He describes how this can appear in review, debugging and design, and recommends stating expectations, examining alternative explanations, requiring concrete verification evidence and keeping changes small enough to understand.

  • O057 · Don't Outsource the Learning
    Addy Osmani · 2026-05-16

    The article separates completing programming tasks from learning the concepts needed to supervise future work. Osmani recommends forming a hypothesis before requesting a fix, asking for explanations before code, reviewing suggestions and periodically reconstructing solutions without assistance. He uses existing research to motivate deliberate learning within ordinary AI-assisted development.

  • O058 · The Orchestration Tax
    Addy Osmani · 2026-05-24

    Osmani describes the human cost of coordinating parallel coding agents as a limit on review and integration. He recommends matching the number of active tasks to review capacity, separating bounded background work from judgment-intensive problems, batching reviews and reserving uninterrupted time for difficult decisions. The goal is to improve accepted work rather than merely increase concurrent activity.

  • O059 · The Intent Debt
    Addy Osmani · 2026-06-05

    The essay distinguishes missing recorded intent from complex code and missing human understanding. Osmani argues that an implementation cannot reliably recover why a consequential choice was made. He recommends keeping goals, constraints and decision rationale in specifications, repository instructions, lightweight decision logs and records of failures so future people and agents can use them.

  • O060 · The New Software Lifecycle
    Addy Osmani · 2026-06-16

    Osmani comments on a Google whitepaper he coauthored, presenting coding agents as models surrounded by tools, context, controls and feedback. He distinguishes always-loaded from task-specific context and separates evaluation of final outputs from evaluation of execution paths. He argues that specification and verification become central constraints as implementation accelerates.

  • O061 · The Agent-Era Career
    Addy Osmani · 2026-07-06

    Osmani’s career advice emphasizes choosing worthwhile problems, developing and demonstrating judgment, and retaining responsibility for delegated work. He recommends deliberate practice, explicit task boundaries, calibrated autonomy and careful completion beyond an agent’s initial draft. The article treats reputation and problem selection as sources of durable career value.

  • O062 · Earning taste and judgment
    Addy Osmani · 2026-07-14

    Osmani argues that delegating routine coding can remove practice through which engineers develop judgment. He recommends deliberate code reading, recording agent mistakes, occasional unaided implementation, deep work in one system, and separate practice in specification and verification. The article connects these habits to career development while synthesizing other authors’ employment and cognition claims.

  • F036 · Citizens Build, Agents Execute, Experts Govern
    Rachel Laycock · 2026-08-19

    Laycock argues that AI-enabled app creation increases the importance of engineering judgment about production reliability, security, operability and maintainability. She uses conversations and a software-engineering workshop to propose a shift in where expertise creates value: people express intent, agents implement, and experienced engineers design the constraints, platforms and feedback that make delivery trustworthy. The proposed division describes responsibilities, rather than fixed job categories.

  • F037 · Making Your Data Ready for Agentic AI
    Pramod Sadalage; Prem Chandrasekaran · 2026-08-27

    Sadalage and Chandrasekaran propose an architecture for data consumed and acted on by agents. It combines validated data contracts, freshness gates, explicit domain and metric definitions, governed capabilities, and observability. They distinguish retrieved material that informs a proposal from deterministic rules that authorize actions, and recommend staged autonomy, delegated access and named ownership. Illustrative pricing, finance and support scenarios explain how these layers fit together.

  • F038 · Maybe We Shouldn't Be Reviewing All This Code
    Rachel Laycock · 2026-09-02

    Laycock argues that rising agent-generated code volume exposes the limits of using pull requests as the main channel for quality, mentoring and shared understanding. She proposes earlier collaborative design, pairing, automated checks and executable architectural constraints, with human review concentrated on consequential or uncertain changes. Her central concern is maintaining understanding of systems while avoiding an expanding queue of diffs.

  • F039 · I don't like LLMs
    Martin Fowler · 2026-09-17

    Fowler separates the practical usefulness of LLMs from his discomfort with their conversational style, confident errors and the values of their developers. The reflection illustrates how trust, professional judgment and personal reactions can shape adoption even when someone recognizes potential technical benefits. It argues for treating agents as software products shaped by organizations rather than autonomous humanlike beings.
