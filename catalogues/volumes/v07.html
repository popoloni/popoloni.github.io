Published title

Subtitle
Reliable Change with Coding Agents
Edition

A practical handbook in which engineers define an outcome, delegate a bounded change to a coding agent, observe the work and decide what can be accepted. A reservation service and a Treasury router carry those decisions into implementation, verification, release and recovery.

5.6.1 identifies the source manuscript. The author's final KDP metadata names this as first commercial edition. The ASIN and Amazon URL were supplied by the author. Printed page numbers in the 7 × 10 English edition; TOC on pp. iii–iv.

Contents

  1. The first accountable change · p. 2

    Tie a Treasury repair to the approved business rule, the candidate under review and the evidence for accepting, rejecting or escalating it.

  2. From need to a verifiable outcome · p. 16

    Investigate a request for a reservation service. Separate assumptions and observable problems, compare interventions, and choose an outcome whose success can be measured.

  3. Specify behavior and acceptance · p. 29

    Use boundary examples, refusals and event sequences to distinguish interpretations of a reservation contract. Turn unresolved questions into decisions for the owner.

  4. Context, memory, tools and agent boundaries · p. 43

    Identify which instructions, permissions and tools a worker actually has. Promote verified claims into memory and revoke or revise them when their source changes.

  5. Design and prototype alternatives · p. 59

    Compare two designs under the same approved behavior, prototype their riskiest assumption and record the conditions for revisiting the design decision.

  6. Plan, delegate and orchestrate · p. 72

    Split work by dependencies and handoffs. Budget attempts, use observations to guide retries and test the integrated result across workers.

  7. Implement and debug a bounded change · p. 88

    Trace a failing value through a bounded change, distinguish fixture and product failures and preserve the candidate for independent review.

  8. Verify the contract independently · p. 103

    Derive expected results independently. Challenge apparently passing checks with properties and controlled faults, and test the actual consumer as well as its provider.

  9. Review, integrate and release with evidence · p. 120

    Bind review claims to the integrated candidate and extracted release artifact. Record who can accept, release or restore it, and recheck assumptions at the point of action.

  10. Evolve with compatible history · p. 136

    Separate replay from reassessment as policies change. Preserve transaction identity, history and compatibility across schema migrations and external effects.

  11. Diagnose the system that actually ran · p. 150

    Distinguish candidate causes of an incident by observing the configuration, policy and ledger actually in use. Retain evidence while restoring service.

  12. Learning, adoption and the whole cost · p. 161

    Evaluate a bounded adoption trial with reviewer effort, quality, cost per accepted outcome and evidence of what maintainers can retain and transfer.
